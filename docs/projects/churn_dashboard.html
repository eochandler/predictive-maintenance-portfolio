TELCO CUSTOMER CHURN

Which customers to save, and what it's actually worth spending to save them

An analysis of 7,043 telecom customers built to answer a retention team's real question: not just "who will churn," but "what's the dollar-optimal way to spend a limited retention budget on them."

The retention budget decision

Three strategies, same 1,761-customer held-out test set. The gap between "do nothing," "use a standard 50% cutoff," and "optimize the cutoff for actual dollar cost" is the entire point of this project.

Do Nothing (no retention program)
Every churner's annual revenue is lost

Cost-Optimized Threshold

Cost vs. decision threshold

Total cost across every possible cutoff. The naive instinct is 0.50 — the actual minimum is well to the left of that, because the cost of a missed churner (their full annual revenue) vastly outweighs the cost of an unnecessary retention offer.

Customer segments

RFM-style segmentation (tenure as loyalty, services subscribed as engagement, monthly charge as value) surfaces four distinct groups — with churn rates ranging from 5% to 47% depending on segment, not evenly distributed.

Churn rate by contract type

The single strongest lever available: month-to-month customers churn at over 15x the rate of two-year contract holders.

Top predictive features

Contract type and tenure dominate — both are things the business can act on directly (incentivizing longer contracts), not just observe.

Methodology

Two models (Logistic Regression, Random Forest) were trained on a 75/25 stratified train/test split and compared by ROC-AUC on the held-out test set; the higher-AUC model's probabilities drive the retention-cost analysis above. Unlike a flat cost matrix, each customer's false-negative cost is their own annual revenue at stake (monthly charge × 12) — a more realistic model of what a retention team actually loses per missed churner. 11 customers with blank billing history (all brand-new, zero-tenure accounts) had their total-charges field set to zero rather than being dropped.
